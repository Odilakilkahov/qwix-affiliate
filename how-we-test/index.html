How we test

This is the method every review and comparison on this site follows. If a published page does not match this method, tell us at /contact and we will fix or remove it.

We use the product ourselves

Before we publish a verdict, someone on our side has signed up for the product, used it for a real task, and recorded a real screen capture of that session. We do not write reviews from a press kit, a landing page, or another site's review. No stock footage and no synthetic voiceover stand in for an actual product walkthrough.

Every price and limit has a source and a date

Pricing, usage caps, and feature limits change often. Each claim we make about them links to the vendor's own pricing or docs page and shows the date we last checked it. If we have not re-checked a number recently, the page will say so instead of quietly going stale.

Every review names who it is not for

No product is right for everyone. Every review includes a dedicated "who it is not for" section with real limitations — not a token caveat buried at the end. If we cannot find a honest case against a tool, we have not tested it carefully enough yet to publish.

Commissions do not change the verdict

We take affiliate commissions from some of the vendors we cover, disclosed on every page that uses them (see our affiliate disclosure). The commission a program pays has no influence on the pros, cons, or "who it is not for" section — those come from using the product, not from the payout.

We revisit pages, not just publish them

When a vendor changes pricing, drops a feature, or we find we were wrong about something, we update the page and move its "updated" date forward. We would rather correct a review in place than leave an outdated one live.
